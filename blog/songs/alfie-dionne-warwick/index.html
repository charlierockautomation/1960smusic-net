Alfie by Dionne Warwick: The Story Behind the 1967 Hit

By Charlie Gillingham · Published November 26, 2026 · Updated November 26, 2026

Alfie by Dionne Warwick reached No. 15 on the Billboard Hot 100 in 1967.
Burt Bacharach and Hal David wrote it for the 1966 film Alfie, and Warwick was their first choice to sing it.
She only recorded it after Cilla Black and Cher had each released a version.

What Is the Song About?

A Film Theme Written in Three Weeks

Bacharach and David were not eager to write it.
Wikipedia says they agreed to submit a song only if they could finish it within three weeks.
Bacharach worked from a rough cut of the film about a Cockney womanizer played by Michael Caine.
David opened with one of Caine's own lines, "What's it all about?"
Bacharach has cited it as his personal favorite of his own songs.

The Golden Rule Line

One line has puzzled listeners for decades: "What will you lend on an old golden rule?"
Songfacts says Barbra Streisand balked at singing it in 1999 rehearsals.
David told her A&R man, "It doesn't mean anything."
Bacharach liked the line, so it stayed.

Who Sang It Before Warwick?

Cilla Black, Abbey Road, Autumn 1965

Bacharach and David wanted Warwick, but Paramount felt the film demanded a British voice.
Sandie Shaw declined, so the song went to Cilla Black.
Black doubted a song named Alfie, since you might call your dog that.
She agreed only if Bacharach arranged it and played piano in London.
He agreed.

The session took place in autumn 1965 at Abbey Road, overseen by George Martin.
Black says she sang eighteen complete takes before Bacharach was satisfied.
Her single came out in January 1966 and reached No. 9 in the UK that May.

Cher and the US Soundtrack

The film's US distributor, United Artists, wanted its own version for the American soundtrack.
It commissioned Cher, and Sonny Bono produced the record.
Her single peaked at No. 32 on the Hot 100 in August 1966.

How Did Warwick Record It?

A Spare Hour at A&R Studios

Warwick's take was an impromptu addition to a session in New York City.
The scheduled tracks for her album Here Where There Is Love were finished early.
Scepter A&R man Steve Tyrell suggested using the leftover time on "Alfie."
Warwick thought it pointless to add to a song with around forty-two recorded versions.
She was persuaded anyway and cut her vocal in a single take.

How a B-Side Became the Hit

Scepter issued it in March 1967 as the flip side of "The Beginning of Loneliness."
Disc jockeys preferred "Alfie" and turned the record over.
Warwick also sang it on the Academy Awards telecast on April 10, 1967, which Wikipedia credits with a major assist.

How High Did Alfie by Dionne Warwick Chart?

The single debuted on the Billboard Hot 100 at No. 95 on the chart dated April 8, 1967.
It climbed steadily and reached No. 15 on the charts dated July 1 and July 8.
Billboard's archive shows 17 weeks on the Hot 100, ending with the chart dated July 29.
Wikipedia says Billboard ranked it No. 44 among the biggest pop hits of 1967.

Why Does It Still Matter?

Awards and Honors

Warwick's recording entered the Grammy Hall of Fame in 2008.
The song was also nominated for the Academy Award for Best Original Song, and lost to "Born Free."

  • Grammy Hall of Fame, Warwick recording, inducted 2008
  • Academy Award nomination, Best Original Song, 39th Academy Awards

Covers and Tributes

On December 3, 2023, Cynthia Erivo sang it to honor Warwick at the Kennedy Center Honors.

Part of a Larger Run

It followed hits such as Walk On By and came a year before Do You Know the Way to San Jose.

FAQ

Who sang Alfie first?

Cilla Black recorded it first, in autumn 1965 at Abbey Road.
Cher sang the version heard in the US release of the film.

What does the line about the old golden rule mean?

Hal David reportedly said it does not mean anything.
He was filling in words for the melody, and Bacharach liked it, so it stayed.

How high did Dionne Warwick's Alfie chart?

It reached No. 15 on the Billboard Hot 100 in 1967.
It also reached No. 5 on the Billboard R&B chart.

Did Alfie win any awards?

Warwick's recording was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame in 2008.
The song was also nominated for the Academy Award for Best Original Song but lost to "Born Free."

Act Naturally: How a Broken Date Became Buck Owens' First No. 1

By Charlie Gillingham · Published November 19, 2026 · Updated November 19, 2026

Act Naturally is the song that gave Buck Owens his first country No. 1, in 1963.
Johnny Russell wrote it after breaking a date, and Capitol released Owens' version on March 11, 1963.
Two years later the Beatles cut it with Ringo Starr on lead vocal.

Who Wrote Act Naturally?

A Broken Date in Fresno

Johnny Russell was a Mississippi native living in Fresno, California, in the early 1960s.
To join friends at a Los Angeles recording session, he had to break a date with his girlfriend.
When she asked why, he said, "They are going to put me in the movies and make a big star out of me."
They both laughed, and he wrote a song around the joke that same day.

Two Years of Closed Doors

His producer passed, saying songs about the movies were not hit material.
"No matter how hard I tried, I couldn't get anyone interested in it," Russell said.
A full two years went by before anyone recorded it.

Why Voni Morrison Shares the Credit

By 1963 Russell was writing with Voni Morrison, who also worked with Owens in Bakersfield.
She heard the song and thought it was a natural for him.
Russell had agreed to share credits, so he gave her partial credit, though Wikipedia says she only submitted it to Owens.
The publishing went to Owens.

How Buck Owens Recorded It

Don Rich Changes Owens's Mind

Owens did not like the song at first.
Don Rich, the Buckaroos' bandleader, heard Russell's demo and liked it.
The tune slowly grew on Owens.
One night Russell got a call asking if Owens could record it, and he said yes.
"I later found out that he had already recorded the song that day and just wanted the publishing rights," Russell said.

The Session and the Release

Owens and the Buckaroos recorded it at Capitol Studios in Hollywood on February 12, 1963.
Ken Nelson produced the session.
Capitol released it as a non-album single on March 11, with "Over and Over Again" on the B-side.

How High Did It Chart?

On June 15, 1963, the record spent the first of four non-consecutive weeks at No. 1.
It stayed on the country chart for 28 weeks in all.
Owens placed 19 singles atop the country chart before the decade ended.

The Beatles and Ringo Starr

A Last-Minute Fix for Help!

The Beatles recorded the song on June 17, 1965, for the UK version of the album Help!
Ringo Starr sang lead, after the band scrapped "If You've Got Trouble" as his song.
Take 13 was the master, and the only one with vocals.

It was the last cover the band recorded until the Get Back sessions in 1969.

A B-Side With a Long Reach

In the US, Capitol held the song off its Help! album and issued it as the B-side of "Yesterday."
It reached No. 47 on the Hot 100 in October 1965, as the 1965 music year guide shows.
Capitol's files listed it as the intended A-side, so Apple gave it the "full apple" side on the 1971 reissue.

The 1989 Duet and Other Covers

Owens and Starr Meet Again

Twenty-four years after the Beatles, Owens and Starr recorded it together.
Capitol released it on July 29, 1989.
It peaked at No. 27 and spent 11 weeks on the country chart, Owens's last top 40 single there.
The duet was nominated for a 1990 Grammy for Best Country Vocal Collaboration.
Starr has played the song on every tour of his All-Starr Band since 1989.

FAQ

Who wrote Act Naturally?

Johnny Russell wrote it.
Voni Morrison shares the writing credit because she pitched the song to Buck Owens, and Owens holds the publishing.

Did Buck Owens or the Beatles record it first?

Buck Owens did.
He recorded it on February 12, 1963, and the Beatles cut their version on June 17, 1965.

How high did Buck Owens' version chart?

It reached No.
1 on Billboard's country chart, with the first of four non-consecutive weeks on June 15, 1963.
It spent 28 weeks on that chart.

Why did the Beatles record it?

Ringo Starr needed a vocal for the album Help!
The band had tried another song for him, were unhappy with it, and replaced it with this one.

A joke made to a girlfriend became a Bakersfield hit and then a Beatles B-side.

The Sound of Silence: How a Flop Became a No. 1 Hit

By Charlie Gillingham · Published October 24, 2026 · Updated October 24, 2026

The Sound of Silence is the Paul Simon song that gave Simon & Garfunkel their first No. 1 hit.
It failed as an acoustic folk track in 1964, then took off after a producer added electric instruments behind their backs.
It topped the Billboard Hot 100 in January 1966.

The Story Behind the Song

A Bathroom With Good Echo

Paul Simon was 21 when he wrote it.
He told Playboy in 1984 that he retreated to the bathroom to play guitar, "because the bathroom had tiles, so it was a slight echo chamber."
Simon finished the lyrics on February 19, 1964.

What the Words Mean

Listeners have long tied it to the Kennedy assassination.
The duo, then performing as Kane & Garr, played it live about two months before that November 1963 shooting, so the link stays speculative.
Simon later said, "I didn't sit down to write a song about alienation in America."
Garfunkel called it a song about "the inability of people to communicate with each other."

The 1964 Acoustic Recording

Simon and Garfunkel cut the first version on March 10, 1964, at Columbia's 7th Avenue studios in New York.
Tom Wilson produced, with two acoustic guitars and an upright bass.
It then appeared, titled "The Sounds of Silence," on Wednesday Morning, 3 A.M., released October 19, 1964.
The album sold about 3,000 copies, and the pair drifted apart.
Simon moved to England and cut a solo version for The Paul Simon Songbook.

The Electric Overdub

Airplay Changes the Plan

In 1965 radio stations in Boston and Florida began spinning the acoustic track.
Wilson had just produced Like a Rolling Stone for Bob Dylan.
On June 15, 1965, he booked session players, including guitarists Al Gorgoni and Vinnie Bell, and added drums, bass and electric guitar to the 1964 take.
Roy Halee engineered.

The Duo Finds Out

Neither Simon nor Garfunkel knew about the session until after the remix was out.
Al Stewart later said Simon was "horrified."
Garfunkel was milder and reportedly said it might sell.
Columbia issued the new mix as a single in September 1965, backed with "We've Got a Groovy Thing Goin'."

The Chart Run

The remix became a top seller in Boston by early November 1965.
It then climbed nationally and reached No. 1 on the Hot 100 for the weeks of January 1 and January 22, 1966.
It traded the top spot with The Beatles' "We Can Work It Out."
The duo regrouped and recorded Sounds of Silence that December with producer Bob Johnston.
Columbia released it on January 17, 1966, and it peaked at No. 21 in the US.

Legacy and Covers

On Screen and in the Charts

  • Director Mike Nichols and editor Sam O'Steen timed early scenes of The Graduate to the song, then licensed it for the 1967 film's opening and closing credits.
  • The Bachelors took a cover to No. 3 in the UK in 1966.
  • Disturbed recorded a hard rock version in 2015 that reached No. 42 on the Hot 100 and No. 1 on Hard Rock Digital Songs.

Where It Fits

The accident of Wilson's overdub helped define folk rock, the blend of folk songwriting and electric instruments led by Dylan and The Byrds.

FAQ

Who wrote The Sound of Silence?

Paul Simon wrote it alone at age 21.
He finished the lyrics on February 19, 1964, after about three months of work, according to Art Garfunkel's recollection of when it began.

What is the song about?

Simon said he did not sit down to write a song about alienation in America.
Garfunkel described it as the inability of people to communicate with each other.

Did Simon and Garfunkel know about the electric overdub?

No.
Producer Tom Wilson added the electric band on June 15, 1965, without telling either of them.
They learned of it after the remix was out.

Did the song reach No. 1?

Yes.
The remixed single hit No. 1 on the Billboard Hot 100 for the weeks of January 1 and January 22, 1966.
It also topped the Cash Box chart.

Who has covered The Sound of Silence?

The Bachelors reached No. 3 in the UK with it in 1966.
Disturbed recorded a hard rock version in 2015 that peaked at No. 42 on the Hot 100.

Few records changed course so completely between one mix and the next.

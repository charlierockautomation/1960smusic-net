Arnold Layne by Pink Floyd: The Story Behind the Debut

By Charlie Gillingham · Published November 26, 2026 · Updated November 26, 2026

Arnold Layne by Pink Floyd was the band's first single, and it peaked at No. 20 in the UK in 1967.
Syd Barrett wrote it about a man who stole women's clothes from washing lines in Cambridge.

What Is the Song About?

A Cambridge Clothesline Thief

The title character is a small-time thief who takes women's clothes from washing lines.
American Songwriter says he was based on a real man living near Cherry Hinton Road in Cambridge.
Barrett and Roger Waters grew up in that city.
The same report says both of their mothers lost clothing to the thief.

Barrett's Own Explanation

Barrett explained the idea in a 1967 Melody Maker interview.
He liked the name first, and he had already written the music.
Then he decided that Arnold "must have a hobby."
That is how a local rumor became the band's debut single.

How Was It Recorded?

Two Studios, Winter 1967

Wikipedia dates the sessions from January 29 to February 27, 1967.
The band worked at Sound Techniques in Chelsea and at EMI Studios in London.
Joe Boyd produced the single.
Columbia released it in the UK under catalogue number DB 8156, backed with "Candy and a Currant Bun."

A Long Live Number, Cut Short

On stage, the song stretched out for ten to fifteen minutes.
Boyd has said the band knew it had to be shortened for a single.
The single version fits the whimsy of psychedelic rock into a tidy pop length.
Sources give the release date as either March 10 or March 11, 1967.

Why Did Radio London Ban It?

The offshore station Radio London refused to play the record.
Its objection was the main character's habit of dressing in women's clothes.
Barrett shrugged this off.
He said the character simply liked dressing up, as many people do.
Reports differ on whether the BBC understood the subject or simply let it through.

How High Did It Chart?

The Official Charts Company lists a peak of No. 20.
It spent eight weeks on the UK chart, from April 5 to May 24, 1967.
Pink Floyd left it off The Piper at the Gates of Dawn, released in August 1967.
The follow-up single, "See Emily Play," did better and reached No. 6.

FAQ

Who wrote Arnold Layne?

Syd Barrett wrote it.
It was Pink Floyd's first single, backed with "Candy and a Currant Bun."

Is Arnold Layne based on a real person?

Reports say the character was based on a small-time thief who stole clothes from washing lines near Cherry Hinton Road in Cambridge.

Why was Arnold Layne banned?

The offshore station Radio London refused to play it because of the character's habit of dressing up in women's clothes.

How high did Arnold Layne chart?

It reached No. 20 on the UK singles chart in 1967.
It spent eight weeks on the chart.

Is Arnold Layne on The Piper at the Gates of Dawn?

No.
The band left both of its first two singles off the debut album, which came out in August 1967.

The song set the template for British psychedelia's blend of oddity and melody, as Wikipedia's entry on the single details.

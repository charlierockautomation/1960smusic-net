Walk On By: Dionne Warwick's 1964 Bacharach Classic

By Charlie Gillingham · Published November 25, 2026 · Updated November 25, 2026

Walk On By is the 1964 Dionne Warwick single in which a woman begs an old flame to pass her on the street without stopping.
Scepter Records released it in April 1964, and it reached No. 6 on the Billboard Hot 100.
It began life as a B-side, and a New York disc jockey helped turn it into a hit.

What Is Walk On By About?

A Meeting on the Street

The song was written by composer Burt Bacharach and lyricist Hal David in 1963 for Dionne Warwick.
The narrator runs into her ex and cannot stop herself from crying every time they meet.
She asks him to keep walking, so he will not see her fall apart.

A Singer Who Excelled at Heartache

Warwick had been singing demos for the pair since 1962, and they learned her voice quickly.
American Songwriter notes that she soared on melancholy material.
Our Dionne Warwick biography traces how the trio came together.

How Was Walk On By Recorded?

A Live Date at Bell Sound

The song was cut at Bell Sound Studios in New York City in late 1963.
It came from the same session as her hit "Anyone Who Had a Heart," Wikipedia says.
Warwick told the Recording Academy that the band played live, with strings, horns, rhythm section and backing singers all in the room.
She remembered a take around the 28th that felt right.

Who Played on It?

Wikipedia lists these session players, though the credits were never printed on the single:

  • Drums: Gary Chester
  • Bass: Russ Savakus
  • Piano: Paul Griffin
  • Organ: Artie Butler

Songfacts adds that Bacharach set up two grand pianos for a richer sound.
He told Record Collector magazine that he knew the song had something.

How Did a B-Side Become a Hit?

Murray the K Turns the Record Over

Scepter first released the record with "Any Old Time of Day" as the featured side.
Warwick recalled that New York disc jockey Murray the K held a contest asking listeners which side they preferred.
They chose the flip side, and only then did radio start playing it.
Songfacts says the label soon had to promote it as the A-side.

From Single to Album

The song appeared on the album Make Way for Dionne Warwick, released August 31, 1964.
Wikipedia notes that the record became her second international million-seller, after "Anyone Who Had a Heart."

How High Did Walk On By Chart?

The single entered the Billboard Hot 100 at No. 85 on the chart dated April 25, 1964.
It climbed to No. 6 and held that spot on the charts dated June 13 and June 20, then fell back over the summer.
Billboard's archive shows 13 weeks on the Hot 100, and it finished 1964 at No. 37 on the year-end list.
Billboard did not publish an R&B chart that year, but Cash Box ranked it No. 1 on its R&B survey.
You can see where it fits in our 1964 music year guide.

In Britain it first charted on April 22, 1964, and Official Charts shows it peaking at No. 9 in mid-May.
That was a strong result for an American singer during the British Invasion.

Why Does It Still Matter?

Isaac Hayes Stretches It Out

Isaac Hayes recorded the song in 1969 for his album Hot Buttered Soul.
His version runs about 12 minutes and turns the ballad into a deep funk vamp.
A shorter single edit reached No. 30 on the Hot 100.

Samples and Stage

Doja Cat sampled Warwick's recording for "Paint the Town Red" in 2023.
That single reached No. 1 on the Hot 100 for the week dated September 16, 2023.
Wikipedia says it was the first time Warwick's voice appeared at No. 1 since "That's What Friends Are For" in 1986.
Warwick herself said she cannot leave the stage without singing it.

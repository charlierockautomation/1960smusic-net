Where Did Our Love Go: The Song That Ended the "No-Hit Supremes"

By Charlie Gillingham · Published October 18, 2026 · Updated October 18, 2026

Where Did Our Love Go is the 1964 single by The Supremes, written by Holland-Dozier-Holland.
Motown released it on June 17, 1964.
It was the group's first No. 1 in the US, with two weeks on top.

The Story Behind Where Did Our Love Go

A Song Meant for the Marvelettes

Holland-Dozier-Holland first wrote the track with the Marvelettes in mind, according to Wikipedia.
Holland and Dozier said the Marvelettes turned it down.
The Marvelettes denied it, saying the slower beat did not suit them.
Lamont Dozier recalled that Gladys Horton called it "the worst thing I ever heard," as quoted by Songfacts.

The No-Hit Supremes

The group was then nicknamed the "No-Hit Supremes."
Ballard said the group wanted a stronger single.
Another objection was that the lyric sounded "kiddie-ish."
Berry Gordy pushed them to record it anyway.
Dozier said the idea came from a breakup.

Recording at Hitsville

The Session

The track was recorded on April 8, 1964, at Hitsville U.S.A. in Detroit.
Brian Holland and Lamont Dozier produced it.
"He Means the World to Me" is on the B-side.
Dozier had cut the backing track in Horton's lower key, so Diana Ross had to sing lower and breathier than usual.
That restrained delivery became the signature of the record.
Dozier had planned intricate backing vocals, but the group refused to learn them, so he simplified them to the repeated chorus.

The Stomp

Songfacts reports the foot stomps were recorded on plywood sheets laid over the floor at Hitsville, picked up by several microphones.
The room's high ceilings added echo.

The Players

  • James Jamerson on bass and Richard "Pistol" Allen on drums.
  • Earl Van Dyke on piano, with Eddie Willis and Robert White on guitar.
  • Mike Terry on baritone sax and Jack Ashford on vibraphone.
  • Diana Ross on lead vocals, with Florence Ballard and Mary Wilson on backing vocals.

Sources differ on some session credits, so this list follows Wikipedia.

How It Charted

In the US

It debuted on the Billboard Hot 100 at No. 77.
It reached No. 1 for two weeks, from August 16 to August 29, 1964, and spent nine weeks in the Top Ten.
It also topped the Hot R&B chart and finished 1964 at No. 10 on the year-end list.
It was the first of five straight Supremes No. 1s, followed by "Baby Love," "Come See About Me," "Stop! In the Name of Love" and "Back in My Arms Again."

Around the World

The record reached No. 3 in the UK and No. 1 in Canada.
Cash Box called it "an infectious handclapping stomp'er."

Legacy and Covers

Beyond the Charts

In August 1965, the song was transmitted to astronauts Gordon Cooper and Pete Conrad aboard Gemini 5, according to Mary Wilson's Library of Congress interview.
Billboard ranks it No. 4 among the greatest girl group songs.

Covers

  • Donnie Elbert took a 1971 version to No. 15 on the US pop chart.
  • Soft Cell paired it with "Tainted Love" in a 1981 medley that hit No. 1 in the UK.

Was this song written for the Marvelettes?

Who plays on this Supremes song?

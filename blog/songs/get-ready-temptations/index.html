Get Ready by The Temptations: Smokey Robinson's Last Song for the Group

By Charlie Gillingham · Published October 25, 2026 · Updated October 25, 2026

Get Ready by The Temptations is the Smokey Robinson song that topped the R&B chart in 1966.
Gordy Records released it on February 7, 1966.
Eddie Kendricks sang lead, and it stalled at No. 29 on the Hot 100.

The Story Behind Get Ready by The Temptations

A Fight Over the First Single of 1966

Motown's Quality Control group chose "Get Ready" as the first Temptations single of 1966.
That meant passing over "Ain't Too Proud to Beg," which Norman Whitfield had produced.
Whitfield was unhappy with the decision.
Berry Gordy agreed that if "Get Ready" was not a major hit, Whitfield's song would come next.

The End of an Era

It was the last song Smokey Robinson wrote and produced for the group.
His earlier hit My Girl had put David Ruffin out front.
This time the spotlight went to Eddie Kendricks and his falsetto.

Recording at Hitsville

The Sessions

According to Wikipedia, the group cut the song in Studio A at Hitsville U.S.A. on December 5, 9 and 29, 1965.
The single runs 2:39.
The B-side is "Fading Away," a Robinson, Pete Moore and Bobby Rogers song that Kendricks also led.
The A-side appears on the album Gettin' Ready.

The Sound

  • Kendricks sings the lead in falsetto, over an up-tempo dance groove.
  • Benny Benjamin drives the track on drums and switches to double-time in the chorus.
  • James Jamerson of the Funk Brothers plays bass, according to Jim Connelly's Certain Songs.
  • The group's bass voice echoes the nursery-rhyme lines in the verses.
  • The other singers answer Kendricks with call-and-response "ahhh" backing vocals.

How It Charted

In the US

The record went to No. 1 on the Hot R&B Singles chart.
On the Billboard Hot 100 it peaked at No. 29, below what Motown expected.
Cash Box also had it at No. 29.
In Canada it reached No. 32 on the RPM chart.

In the UK

British fans took to it more warmly, and it reached No. 10 on the UK Singles Chart.
The BPI later certified it Silver.

What Happened Next

Gordy kept his word and released "Ain't Too Proud to Beg" next.
Ruffin sang lead, and the record reached No. 13 on the pop chart.
Whitfield then replaced Robinson as the group's producer.

Rare Earth and Other Covers

The Rare Earth Version

The Motown band Rare Earth had first recorded the song for their 1968 album Dreams/Answers.
Their 1970 single came out on February 18 and became the bigger pop hit.
It reached No. 4 on the Hot 100 and No. 2 on Cash Box, and the RIAA certified it Gold.
The album version of their Get Ready runs 21:32, while the single runs 2:46.
DJ Kool Herc routinely used that record in his turntable sets.

More Recordings

  • The Supremes covered it on The Supremes A' Go-Go in 1966.
  • Ella Fitzgerald recorded it in 1969 and sang it on The Carol Burnett Show that November.
  • Smokey Robinson cut a disco version for his 1979 album Where There's Smoke....
  • The Proclaimers recorded it for the Dumb and Dumber soundtrack in 1994.

Where It Fits

Who sang lead on Get Ready?

When did Get Ready come out?

Gordy Records released it on February 7, 1966.

Did Get Ready hit No. 1?

It reached No. 1 on the Hot R&B Singles chart but only No. 29 on the Billboard Hot 100.

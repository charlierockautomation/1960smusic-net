Come See About Me: The Supremes Hit Motown Rushed Out to Beat a Cover

By Charlie Gillingham · Published October 18, 2026 · Updated October 18, 2026

Come See About Me is the 1964 single by The Supremes, written by Holland-Dozier-Holland.
Motown released it on October 27, 1964.
It was the group's third straight No. 1 in the US.

The Story Behind Come See About Me

A Cover That Forced Motown's Hand

The Supremes cut the song first, as part of the sessions that followed their breakout hit.
It sat on their August 1964 album Where Did Our Love Go.
Then Wand Records got hold of it.
Wand put out a close copy sung by a young singer named Nella Dodds in October 1964.
Dodds' single reached only No. 74 on the Hot 100, according to Wikipedia.
Motown answered by rushing out the Supremes' own version, which hurt her sales.

A Sad Lyric on a Bouncy Beat

The singer has lost her man and asks him to come back to her.
She sounds sure she can win him over.
Kele Okereke of Bloc Party has said the words carry a sad weight while the music stays bouncy.
That gap between the lyric and the stomping beat is a big part of the record's pull.

Recording at Hitsville

The Team

Holland-Dozier-Holland wrote the song, and Brian Holland and Lamont Dozier produced it.
The track was cut at Hitsville U.S.A. in Detroit.
It came out of the same two-week stretch that produced "Baby Love."
Diana Ross sang lead, with Florence Ballard and Mary Wilson on the call-and-response parts.

The Funk Brothers

The band was Motown's house crew, the Funk Brothers.
The lineup on this record is listed as:

  • Earl Van Dyke on piano
  • Joe Messina on guitar
  • James Jamerson on bass
  • Uriel Jones on drums
  • Jack Ashford on vibraphone
  • Hank Cosby on tenor sax and Mike Terry on baritone sax

Handclaps land on every beat, which gives the song its stomping Detroit feel.

How It Charted

In the US

Billboard praised its "pronounced Detroit beat, steady and exacting" in November 1964.
Cash Box called it a "pulsating stomp" and said the group was in top-of-the-chart form.
The single hit No. 1 on the Hot 100 in December 1964.
The Beatles' "I Feel Fine" then took over, and the Supremes returned to the top in January 1965.
It was the third of five straight No. 1 singles for the group.
It also topped the Cash Box Top 100.

On Television

The Supremes sang it on The Ed Sullivan Show on December 27, 1964.
That was the first of their 17 live appearances on the show.

Around the World

It reached No. 1 in Canada.
In the UK it peaked at No. 27.

Legacy and Covers

Covers

Plenty of artists have returned to the song:

  • Junior Walker and the All Stars cut a saxophone-led version that reached the pop top 25.
  • Neil Sedaka named a 1984 album after it, with Mary Wilson singing backup, per Songfacts.
  • Martina McBride recorded it for her 2014 covers album Everlasting.
  • The cast of Glee sang it in a 2012 Thanksgiving episode.

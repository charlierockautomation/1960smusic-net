I Want to Hold Your Hand: How The Beatles Broke America

"I Want to Hold Your Hand" is the single that turned The Beatles from a British sensation into an American one.
Written in an afternoon and recorded in October 1963, it became their first US number one and the song playing the day America truly noticed them.

How The Beatles Wrote "I Want to Hold Your Hand"

John Lennon and Paul McCartney wrote "I Want to Hold Your Hand" face to face, on a piano in the basement of Jane Asher's parents' house on Wimpole Street in London.

Lennon later described the method as "eyeball to eyeball," the two of them working out the song's key change in the same room, in real time, rather than trading finished verses.

The First Beatles Song Recorded on Four Tracks

The Beatles recorded "I Want to Hold Your Hand" on October 17, 1963, and it was the first Beatles song made using four-track recording equipment, giving producer George Martin more room to layer the band's vocals and instruments than earlier sessions had allowed.
It took 17 takes to finish.

A UK Number One Delayed by Their Own Record

"I Want to Hold Your Hand" was released in the UK on November 29, 1963, backed with "This Boy."

It sold over a million copies on advance orders alone, enough to top the charts outright, except The Beatles' own "She Loves You" was still charting and blocked it for two weeks.
Once it dislodged its predecessor, "I Want to Hold Your Hand" held UK number one for five weeks.

The US Release That Started Beatlemania

Capitol Records released "I Want to Hold Your Hand" in the US on December 26, 1963, after earlier American labels had passed on The Beatles entirely.

250,000 Copies in Three Days

The single sold 250,000 copies in its first three days on sale, with reports of 10,000 copies an hour moving through record stores in New York City at its peak.
It reached number one on the Billboard Hot 100 on February 1, 1964, The Beatles' first US chart-topper, and stayed there for seven weeks.

Ed Sullivan and the Song America Already Knew

By the time The Beatles stepped onto The Ed Sullivan Show stage on February 9, 1964, in front of an estimated 73 million viewers, "I Want to Hold Your Hand" had already been the country's top single for over a week.

The band played it again on Sullivan's show the following week, on February 16, by which point the song had done more than top a chart.
It had proven a British band could dominate American radio, television, and record stores all at once, opening the door for the wider British Invasion that followed.

FAQ: I Want to Hold Your Hand

When was I Want to Hold Your Hand released?

I Want to Hold Your Hand was released in the UK on November 29, 1963.
It followed on Capitol Records in the US on December 26, 1963.

Who wrote I Want to Hold Your Hand?

I Want to Hold Your Hand was written by John Lennon and Paul McCartney, face to face at a piano in the basement of Jane Asher's parents' house on Wimpole Street in London.

Was I Want to Hold Your Hand The Beatles' first US number one?

Yes.
I Want to Hold Your Hand reached number one on the Billboard Hot 100 on February 1, 1964, The Beatles' first US chart-topper, and held the spot for seven weeks.

How does I Want to Hold Your Hand connect to the Ed Sullivan Show?

The Beatles performed I Want to Hold Your Hand live on The Ed Sullivan Show on February 9, 1964, in front of an estimated 73 million viewers, and again the following week on February 16.

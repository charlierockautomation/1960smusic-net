Dionne Warwick: The Voice of Bacharach and David

By Charlie Gillingham · Published November 24, 2026 · Updated November 24, 2026

Dionne Warwick was the singer who carried Burt Bacharach and Hal David's hardest songs onto the 1960s charts.
From 1962 to 1971 she recorded for Scepter Records, and "Walk On By" and "I Say a Little Prayer" became standards.

From a Gospel Choir to the Demo Studio

A Church Family

Marie Dionne Warrick was born in East Orange, New Jersey, on December 12, 1940.
Her mother managed the Drinkard Singers, a family gospel group.
Her aunt Cissy Houston trained the girls' voices.
At fourteen she and her sister Dee Dee joined the Gospelaires, a spinoff of that group.
The Gospelaires became busy backing singers on sessions for the Drifters, Dinah Washington, Solomon Burke and Chuck Jackson.
She kept the session work going while she studied at the Hartt College of Music in West Hartford, Connecticut, on a scholarship.

Bacharach Hears a Backup Singer

At a Drifters session for "Mexican Divorce," composer Burt Bacharach noticed her voice.
He hired her to record demos of songs he wrote with lyricist Hal David, at $12.50 a session.
One demo, "It's Love That Really Counts," reached Scepter Records president Florence Greenberg.
According to Current Biography, Greenberg told Bacharach, "Forget the song, get the girl!"
The singer signed with Bacharach and David's production company, which in turn signed with Scepter in 1962.

Signing With Scepter

A Title Born in Anger

She had recorded the demo of "Make It Easy on Yourself" and wanted it as her first single.
Then she learned the song had gone to Jerry Butler.
She snapped the phrase "don't make me over" at Bacharach and David in anger.
They built a song from it, and she recorded it the next day.
"Don't Make Me Over" came out in November 1962 and reached No. 21 on the Hot 100 and No. 5 on the R&B chart.
The label misspelled Warrick as Warwick, and she kept the new spelling.

How Did She Break Through in 1964?

Anyone Who Had a Heart

Her next two singles barely cracked the Hot 100.
Then came "Anyone Who Had a Heart," released in November 1963.
It reached No. 8, her first top 10 hit and an international million-seller.
In Britain, Cilla Black's cover of the song went to No. 1.
Warwick felt insulted that UK executives wanted her songs sung by someone else.

Dionne Warwick and the British Invasion

"Walk On By" followed in April 1964 and reached No. 6 on the Hot 100.
She held her own against British Invasion acts better than most American artists.
Cash Box named her the bestselling female vocalist of 1964.
In 1965 she recorded "Mr. Kiss Kiss Bang Bang" for the James Bond film Thunderball.
The producer insisted the theme carry the film's title, so Tom Jones recorded "Thunderball" instead.

The Peak Years, 1966 to 1969

When a B-Side Became a Standard

"Message to Michael" reached No. 8 in the spring of 1966.
The album Here Where There Is Love followed in January 1967 and became her first gold record.
One of its tracks, "Alfie," turned into a radio hit when disc jockeys played the album cut.
"I Say a Little Prayer" began as a B-side too, and it climbed to No. 4.
It was the first RIAA-certified million-selling single for Bacharach and David.
Its flip side, the "Theme from Valley of the Dolls" by André and Dory Previn, became a hit of its own when the film opened in early 1968.

San Jose and a Grammy

"Do You Know the Way to San Jose" reached No. 10 in 1968, and it made the top 10 in the US, UK and Canada.
It won her first Grammy, for Best Contemporary Pop Vocal Performance, Female, in 1969.
Copper magazine notes that she never liked the song.
Next came "This Girl's in Love with You" at No. 7 and "I'll Never Fall in Love Again" at No. 6.
The second song came from the Bacharach and David stage musical Promises, Promises.

After Scepter: Warner, Arista and Honors

Leaving the Hit Factory

In 1971 she left Scepter for Warner on a $5 million contract.
Variety called it the richest deal given to a female vocalist up to that time.
The film Lost Horizon flopped in 1972, and Bacharach and David ended their partnership.
Her Warner albums missed the top 40, and her one big hit of the decade was "Then Came You" with the Spinners.
That record reached No. 1 on October 26, 1974.

Comeback and Legacy

At Arista, "I'll Never Love This Way Again" reached No. 5 in 1979.
In 1985 she reunited with Bacharach for "That's What Friends Are For," a No. 1 hit.
Before "Don't Make Me Over," few Black women had charted on the pop side other than Dinah Washington.
She received the Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award in 2019.
In 2024 the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame inducted her in the Musical Excellence category.
Her breakthrough came in 1963 and 1964, and her biggest years ran from 1967 through 1968 to 1969.

FAQ

Who wrote the biggest hits of Dionne Warwick?

Burt Bacharach and Hal David wrote most of her 1960s hits.
The Valley of the Dolls theme came from André and Dory Previn.

What was the first hit by Dionne Warwick?

"Don't Make Me Over," released in November 1962, reached No. 21 on the Billboard Hot 100 and No. 5 on the R&B chart.

What was her highest-charting single of the 1960s?

"I Say a Little Prayer" peaked at No. 4 on the Hot 100 in 1967.

Was Whitney Houston related to Dionne Warwick?

Yes.
Whitney Houston was her cousin, the daughter of Warwick's aunt Cissy Houston.

Is Dionne Warwick in the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame?

Yes.
The Rock and Roll Hall of Fame inducted her in 2024 in the Musical Excellence category.

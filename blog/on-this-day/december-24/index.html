The Beatles Christmas Show opens at the Finsbury Park Astoria

The Beatles opened The Beatles Christmas Show at the Astoria in Finsbury Park, London, on December 24, 1963, on a bill with Cilla Black, Billy J. Kramer with the Dakotas, the Fourmost and Tommy Quickly, per the Paul McCartney Project.

Disc's review called it a two-hour show, staged at the height of British Beatlemania, and the clip below is I Want to Hold Your Hand, then No. 1 in Britain.

Nashville street singer Cortelia Clark dies

Cortelia Clark, the blind Nashville street singer whose only album, Blues in the Street, won the 1967 Grammy for best folk recording, died on Christmas Eve 1969 after weeks in hospital, per the Country Music Hall of Fame and Museum.

Producer Felton Jarvis had recorded the album on the street where Clark played, and the clip below is the museum's own short film on his story.

Jerry Garcia jams on Christmas Eve at the Matrix

Jerry Garcia played the Matrix in San Francisco on December 24, 1968, in a show billed as the Harvey Mandel Group, per the official Jerry Garcia show archive.

Garcia was still a full-time member of the Grateful Dead, and the clip below is the Dead's Dark Star from their official catalog.

Tommy James records I Think We're Alone Now on Christmas Eve

Tommy James and the Shondells recorded I Think We're Alone Now at Allegro Studios in New York on December 24, 1966, so it could come out early in the new year, per Parke Puterbaugh's liner notes for Roulette's 1989 Anthology.

Produced by Ritchie Cordell and Bo Gentry, it reached No. 4 on the Hot 100 in April 1967, and the clip below is the original recording.

Winchester Cathedral holds No. 1 as I'm a Believer closes in

The New Vaudeville Band's Winchester Cathedral was No. 1 on the Billboard Hot 100 dated December 24, 1966, its third week on top, with Donovan's Mellow Yellow at No. 2 and the Monkees' I'm a Believer at No. 3.

I'm a Believer took over the top spot the next week, and the clip below is Geoff Stephens's novelty hit.

The Monkees' debut album spends a seventh week at No. 1

The Monkees' first album, The Monkees, was No. 1 on the Billboard 200 dated December 24, 1966, its seventh week on top, ahead of the Doctor Zhivago soundtrack.

The album included Last Train to Clarksville, the TV band's first No. 1 single, and the clip below is that song from the Monkees' official channel.

The Temptations reach No. 1 on the R&B chart with (I Know) I'm Losing You

The Temptations' (I Know) I'm Losing You climbed from No. 3 to No. 1 on Billboard's R&B singles chart dated December 24, 1966, ahead of the Supremes' You Keep Me Hangin' On.

David Ruffin sang the lead on the Norman Whitfield production, and the clip below is the original Motown single.

Don Cherry records Complete Communion for Blue Note

Cornetist Don Cherry recorded Complete Communion with Gato Barbieri, Henry Grimes and Ed Blackwell at Van Gelder Studio in Englewood Cliffs, New Jersey, on December 24, 1965, per the jazzdisco Van Gelder Studio catalog.

Blue Note issued it in 1966 as Cherry's first album as a leader for the label, and the clip below is its title suite, a landmark of free jazz.

Another Beatles Christmas Show opens at the Hammersmith Odeon

The Beatles opened Another Beatles Christmas Show at the Hammersmith Odeon in London on December 24, 1964, with Freddie and the Dreamers, Elkie Brooks and the Yardbirds, then featuring Eric Clapton, on the bill, per the Paul McCartney Project.

The run lasted until January 16, 1965, and the clip below is She's a Woman, the B-side of I Feel Fine.

I Feel Fine gives the Beatles the Christmas No. 1 in Britain

The Beatles' I Feel Fine was No. 1 on the UK chart dated December 24, 1964, its fourth week on the chart, per the Official Charts Company.

Petula Clark's Downtown sat at No. 2, and the clip below is the Beatles' official clip for I Feel Fine.

Wayne Shorter records Speak No Evil at Van Gelder Studio

Wayne Shorter recorded Speak No Evil with Freddie Hubbard, Herbie Hancock, Ron Carter and Elvin Jones at Van Gelder Studio in Englewood Cliffs, New Jersey, on December 24, 1964, per the jazzdisco Van Gelder Studio catalog.

Blue Note released the album in 1966, and the clip below is its title track, a staple of 1960s jazz.

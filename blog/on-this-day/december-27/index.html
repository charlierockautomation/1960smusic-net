Diana Ross & the Supremes hit No. 1 with Someday We'll Be Together

Diana Ross & the Supremes' Someday We'll Be Together moved from No. 2 to No. 1 on the Billboard Hot 100 dated December 27, 1969, displacing Peter, Paul and Mary's Leaving on a Jet Plane.

It held the top spot for one week and was replaced on the January 3, 1970 chart by B.J. Thomas, which makes it the last Hot 100 No. 1 dated in the 1960s, and the clip below is the single version from the group's Topic channel.

Led Zeppelin II reaches No. 1 on the Billboard 200

Led Zeppelin's second album moved from No. 2 to No. 1 on the Billboard 200 dated December 27, 1969, its first week on top after debuting at No. 199 on the chart dated November 8.

It was the band's first No. 1 album, and the clip below is Whole Lotta Love, the album's opening track, from the band's official channel.

Leonard Cohen releases Songs of Leonard Cohen

Columbia released Leonard Cohen's debut album, Songs of Leonard Cohen, on December 27, 1967, per Cohen's official site.

Paul McCartney defends Magical Mystery Tour on The Frost Programme

Paul McCartney appeared on David Frost's television show on December 27, 1967, the day after the Magical Mystery Tour film aired on BBC1, to explain it, per the Beatles Bible.

The interview was filmed in the early evening and broadcast the same night, and the clip below is Your Mother Should Know, a song from the film's soundtrack.

The Supremes make their first appearance on The Ed Sullivan Show

The Supremes first appeared on The Ed Sullivan Show on December 27, 1964, performing Come See About Me, per the official Ed Sullivan site.

The Times names Lennon and McCartney the outstanding composers of 1963

The Times of London ran music critic William Mann's essay "What Songs the Beatles Sang" on December 27, 1963, calling John Lennon and Paul McCartney "the outstanding English composers of 1963," per the Beatles Bible.

Mann singled out the Aeolian cadence at the end of Not a Second Time, and the clip below is that song from the Beatles' official catalog.

The Beatles play the third night of their Christmas Show in Finsbury Park

The Beatles played the third night of The Beatles' Christmas Show at the Astoria Cinema in Finsbury Park, London, on December 27, 1963, closing a bill that included Cilla Black and Billy J. Kramer and the Dakotas, per the Beatles Bible.

Their 25-minute set included I Want to Hold Your Hand, and the clip below is that song from the band's official catalog.

The Beatles play the ninth night of their last Star-Club residency

The Beatles played the Star-Club in Hamburg on December 27, 1962, the ninth date of a residency that ran from December 18 to December 31, per the Beatles Bible.

It was their third and final Star-Club stay, and the clip below is Love Me Do, the debut single they had released that October.

The Beatles play The Beatles' Xmas Party at the Cavern Club

The Beatles played an evening show at the Cavern Club in Liverpool on December 27, 1961, advertised as "The Beatles' Xmas Party," with Gerry and the Pacemakers and Kingsize Taylor and the Dominoes also on the bill, per the Beatles Bible.

Gerry and the Pacemakers went on to a No. 1 UK single with How Do You Do It in 1963, and the clip below is that song from the group's Topic channel.

The Beatles play their breakthrough Welcome Home show at Litherland Town Hall

The Beatles played Litherland Town Hall near Liverpool on December 27, 1960, on a bill that also included the Searchers, with Chas Newby filling in on bass, per the Beatles Bible.

The show opened with Little Richard's Long Tall Sally and cemented the band as Liverpool's top live draw, and the clip below is the band's own recording of that song.

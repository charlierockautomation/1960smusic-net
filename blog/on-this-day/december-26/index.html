Capitol releases I Want to Hold Your Hand in the United States

Capitol Records released the Beatles' I Want to Hold Your Hand in the United States on December 26, 1963, backed with I Saw Her Standing There, per the Beatles Bible.

Capitol moved the single up from its planned January date after a Washington, D.C. disc jockey began playing a British import, and the clip below is the song from the band's official catalog.

The Grateful Dead play McFarlin Auditorium in Dallas

Led Zeppelin play their first North American show in Denver

Led Zeppelin made their North American concert debut at Denver's Auditorium Arena on December 26, 1968, an unadvertised addition to a bill with Vanilla Fudge and Spirit, per Colorado Public Radio.

The tour that began that night ran to February 16, 1969, and the clip below is Good Times Bad Times, the opening track of the debut album the band was promoting.

Magical Mystery Tour premieres on BBC1

The Beatles' Magical Mystery Tour film was shown on BBC1 on Boxing Day, December 26, 1967, in black and white, per the Beatles Bible.

The film drew mixed reviews, a colour showing followed on BBC2 on January 5, 1968, and the clip below is the title song from the band's official catalog.

The Beatles hit No. 1 with I Feel Fine

The Beatles' I Feel Fine moved from No. 2 to No. 1 on the Billboard Hot 100 dated December 26, 1964, displacing the Supremes' Come See About Me.

The B-side, She's a Woman, climbed from No. 14 to No. 4 on the same chart, and the clip below is the A-side from the band's Vevo channel.

The Kinks debut on the Hot 100 with All Day and All of the Night

The Kinks' All Day and All of the Night entered the Billboard Hot 100 dated December 26, 1964 at No. 69.

It reached No. 7 in 1965, and the clip below is the official audio from the band's channel.

The Shangri-Las debut with Give Him a Great Big Kiss

The Shangri-Las' Give Him a Great Big Kiss entered the Billboard Hot 100 dated December 26, 1964 at No. 83, and their Maybe entered the same chart at No. 93.

Give Him a Great Big Kiss peaked at No. 18 in early 1965, and the clip below is the single from the group's catalog.

Elvis Presley spends a fifth week at No. 1 with Are You Lonesome To-night?

1969

The Beatles release Something backed with Come Together in the US

Apple Records issued the Beatles' double A-side single Something, backed with Come Together, across the United States on October 6, 1969 as Apple 2654, pulling the first two songs from that fall's Abbey Road.

It marked the first time a George Harrison composition took top billing on a Beatles single, and the pairing went on to top the Billboard Hot 100 that November.

1968
broadcast

Hey Jude makes its US TV debut on the Smothers Brothers Comedy Hour

CBS aired the Beatles' promotional film for Hey Jude on The Smothers Brothers Comedy Hour, the song's first American television appearance, about a month after its UK debut on Frost on Sunday.

Michael Lindsay-Hogg's film, shot at Twickenham with a studio audience singing along through the coda, reached millions of US viewers during the single's nine-week run at No. 1.

release

Tony Bennett releases his first Christmas album, Snowfall

Columbia Records released Tony Bennett's Snowfall: The Tony Bennett Christmas Album on October 6, 1968, his first holiday record, arranged and conducted by Robert Farnon.

Rooted in the same jazz and easy listening tradition Bennett built his career on, the album later won a Grammy for Best Engineered Recording (Non-Classical) and became a seasonal standard, led by its title track.

1967
industry

Jimi Hendrix and Stevie Wonder jam together at the BBC

The Jimi Hendrix Experience were at the BBC's Playhouse Theatre in London recording for the radio show Top Gear when 17-year-old Stevie Wonder, there for an interview, sat in on drums for an impromptu cover of his own song I Was Made to Love Her.

Mitch Mitchell had stepped out when Wonder took the kit, turning a routine session into a cross-genre jam that later surfaced officially on the Experience's BBC Sessions album.

industry

The Beatles finish Blue Jay Way with a final overdub

The Beatles returned to Studio Two at EMI Studios to complete George Harrison's Blue Jay Way for Magical Mystery Tour, filling the last open track on the four-track tape with a cello and tambourine overdub.

Built from a single droning take, backward loops and Harrison's vocal run through a Leslie speaker, it stayed one of the group's most overtly psychedelic studio experiments.

culture

San Francisco's Diggers stage the Death of the Hippie funeral

The Diggers led a mock funeral procession from the Psychedelic Shop through Haight-Ashbury to Buena Vista Park, mourning the invented death of Hippie as a label the media had turned into a costume.

The staged funeral was organizers' own symbolic close to the Summer of Love, urging the crowds who had flooded the neighborhood to carry the movement back to their own hometowns instead, the same message Scott McKenzie's San Francisco had broadcast to the country that summer.

1966
concert

Rolling Stones headline Birmingham Odeon with Ike & Tina Turner and the Yardbirds

The Rolling Stones played two shows at the Birmingham Odeon on their UK package tour, sharing the bill with Ike & Tina Turner and fellow British Invasion openers the Yardbirds.

The tour arrived just weeks after the Stones' single Have You Seen Your Mother, Baby, Standing in the Shadow? hit the charts, the song that carried the night's headline set.

1964
concert

Miles Davis' new quintet plays the first Helsinki Jazz Festival

Miles Davis brought his new band, saxophonist Wayne Shorter, pianist Herbie Hancock, bassist Ron Carter and drummer Tony Williams, to Messuhalli in Helsinki for the inaugural Helsinki Jazz Festival, a concert Finnish broadcaster YLE recorded for radio.

The gig came only weeks after Shorter joined the band, catching Davis's celebrated jazz and easy listening quintet's exploratory chemistry forming in real time.

concert

Dave Brubeck Quartet debuts Unisphere at the same Helsinki festival

Later on that same bill at Messuhalli, the Dave Brubeck Quartet, Brubeck with Paul Desmond, Eugene Wright and Joe Morello, performed their original composition Unisphere for the Helsinki Jazz Festival crowd.

Miles Davis and Dave Brubeck sharing one Finnish stage the same night made the festival's debut a genuine jazz summit, previewed here with the quartet's best-known tune, Take Five.

1963
concert

The Beatles play two sold-out shows at Kirkcaldy's Carlton Cinema

The Beatles performed two houses at the Carlton Cinema in Kirkcaldy, Scotland, each drawing roughly 1,500 fans during their autumn Scottish mini-tour, with support act Houston Wells and the Marksmen on the bill.

The Fife town left enough of a mark on John Lennon that he later wrote it into the White Album's Cry Baby Cry, and the band was touring that week behind the summer's UK hit She Loves You.

1962
concert

Gerry Mulligan Quartet plays the Paris Olympia for Norman Granz

The Gerry Mulligan Quartet, baritone saxophonist Gerry Mulligan with Bob Brookmeyer, Bill Crow and Gus Johnson, played the Paris Olympia in a concert produced by Norman Granz and later issued on Pablo Records.

The set captured the quartet's cool jazz and easy listening interplay live in Europe, echoed here by Walkin' Shoes, one of the pianoless quartet's signature tunes.

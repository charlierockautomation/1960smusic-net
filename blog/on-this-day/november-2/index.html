The Band play Up On Cripple Creek on Ed Sullivan

Stevie Wonder's For Once in My Life enters the Hot 100

Stevie Wonder's For Once in My Life entered the Billboard Hot 100 at No. 79 on the chart dated November 2, 1968, per Billboard.

It went on to peak at No. 2 on both the Hot 100 and the R&B chart, and the clip below is the single.

Cream get a platinum disc at Madison Square Garden

Cream received a platinum disc for Wheels of Fire at their Madison Square Garden show on November 2, 1968, during their farewell US tour, per Ritchie Yorke.

The award marked a million sales of the double album, and the clip below is White Room from it.

The Beatles finish Hello, Goodbye at Abbey Road

The Beatles added the final overdub to Hello, Goodbye on November 2, 1967, a second bass part from Paul McCartney in Studio Three at Abbey Road, per the Beatles Bible.

The song was then mixed in mono in six attempts, with the last chosen as the master, and the clip below is the finished single.

Motown releases the Temptations' (I Know) I'm Losing You

The Temptations' (I Know) I'm Losing You came out on Motown's Gordy label on November 2, 1966, per Wikipedia.

Norman Whitfield produced it and it reached No. 1 on the R&B chart and No. 8 on the Hot 100, and the clip below is the single, a Motown classic.

The Beatles play two late-added shows in Belfast

The Beatles played two shows at King's Hall in Belfast on November 2, 1964, a late booking by promoter Arthur Howes for what had been a rest day, per the Beatles Bible.

It was their second and last visit to Northern Ireland, and the clip below is A Hard Day's Night, which was in the 10-song set.

k.d. lang is born in Edmonton

k.d. lang was born Kathryn Dawn Lang in Edmonton, Alberta, on November 2, 1961, and grew up in Consort, Alberta, per Wikipedia.

She became a Grammy-winning pop and country singer-songwriter, and the clip below is Constant Craving.

Kai Winding and J.J. Johnson cut tracks for Impulse!

Kai Winding and J. J. Johnson recorded three tracks for The Great Kai & J. J. at Van Gelder Studio in Englewood Cliffs on November 2, 1960, with Bill Evans on piano, per Wikipedia.

The album became the first release on Impulse! Records, and the clip below is Blue Monk, one of the jazz tracks cut that day.

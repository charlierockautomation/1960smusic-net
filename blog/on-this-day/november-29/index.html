1969 in 1960s music: the full year guide

The Beatles hit No. 1 with Come Together / Something

The Beatles reached No. 1 on the Billboard Hot 100 in the chart dated November 29, 1969, with the double-sided single Come Together / Something, per Wikipedia.

Billboard had begun listing two-sided singles once instead of ranking each side, it was the Beatles' 18th Hot 100 No. 1, and the clip below is Come Together.

The Rolling Stones play two shows at Boston Garden

The Rolling Stones played two shows at Boston Garden on November 29, 1969, the last scheduled dates of their American tour, per Rolling Stones Data.

The Harvard Crimson reported more than 30,000 seats sold out within hours, and the clip below is Honky Tonk Women, their 1969 single.

Apple releases Two Virgins in the UK

John Lennon and Yoko Ono's album Unfinished Music No. 1: Two Virgins came out in the UK on November 29, 1968, just over two weeks after its US release, per the Beatles Bible.

Distributors sold the LP in a plain brown wrapper to hide the nude cover, and the clip below is Give Peace a Chance, the Plastic Ono Band single from 1969.

The Beatles edit Christmas Time (Is Here Again)

The Beatles edited Christmas Time (Is Here Again) in the Studio One control room at EMI Studios on November 29, 1967, with George Martin and Geoff Emerick, per the Beatles Bible.

It was their fifth fan club Christmas record, edited a day after they recorded it, and the clip below is Hello, Goodbye, the single they had just released.

The Beatles record take 7 of Strawberry Fields Forever

The Beatles returned to Studio Two at Abbey Road on November 29, 1966, and retook the arrangement they had begun the day before, per Wikipedia and the Beatles Bible.

The better of that day's takes became take 7, which took overdubs and fills the first minute of the finished record, and the clip below is the finished song.

1960 in 1960s music: the full year guide

Paul McCartney and Pete Best are arrested in Hamburg

Paul McCartney and Pete Best were arrested in Hamburg on November 29, 1960, after club owner Bruno Koschmider reported them for attempted arson at the Bambi Kino, per the Beatles Bible.

Accounts differ on what they set alight, they were deported the next day, and the clip below is Love Me Do, which the Beatles released less than two years later.

John Lennon and Yoko Ono meet Pierre Trudeau in Ottawa

The Beatles' John Lennon and Yoko Ono met Prime Minister Pierre Trudeau in his Centre Block office on Parliament Hill in Ottawa on December 23, 1969, a talk the Ottawa Citizen said was booked for 10 or 15 minutes and ran 50, per the Ottawa Beatles Site.

Lennon called Trudeau "a beautiful person" afterward, and the clip below is Happy Xmas (War Is Over), the 1971 song named for the WAR IS OVER! poster campaign the couple were running that month.

John Lennon plays Father Christmas at Apple's chaotic children's party

John Lennon and Yoko Ono dressed as Father and Mother Christmas to hand out presents at the Beatles' Apple Christmas party for staff children at 3 Savile Row, London, on December 23, 1968, per the day-by-day account at They May Be Parted, which draws on Beatles Book and Disc and Music Echo reports.

Hells Angels whom George Harrison had invited to London stripped the 43-pound turkey before it reached the table, and the clip below is Hey Jude, the Beatles' first single on Apple that year.

Bobby Hatfield cuts Only You for Verve

Bobby Hatfield of the Righteous Brothers recorded Only You and I've Got My Eyes on You in Los Angeles on December 23, 1968, per the jazzdisco Verve Records discography.

Hatfield was making solo records for Verve after the duo's split that year, and the clip below is the single version of Only You.

The Monkees hold No. 1 on the singles and albums charts at once

The Monkees' Daydream Believer was No. 1 on the Billboard Hot 100 dated December 23, 1967, its fourth week on top, while Pisces, Aquarius, Capricorn & Jones Ltd. led the Billboard 200 the same week.

The Beatles' Hello Goodbye took over the top of the Hot 100 the following week, and the clip below is the Monkees' official video for Daydream Believer.

chart

Gladys Knight and the Pips top the R&B chart with I Heard It Through the Grapevine

Gladys Knight and the Pips' I Heard It Through the Grapevine was No. 1 on Billboard's R&B singles chart dated December 23, 1967, with Smokey Robinson and the Miracles' I Second That Emotion at No. 2.

It was also No. 2 on the Hot 100 that week, a year before Marvin Gaye's version took the song to No. 1, and the clip below is Knight's Motown recording.

chart

Bill Anderson and Jan Howard reach No. 1 in country with For Loving You

Bill Anderson and Jan Howard's duet For Loving You climbed from No. 4 to No. 1 on Billboard's country singles chart dated December 23, 1967.

It replaced Jack Greene's What Locks the Door at the top, and the clip below is the original country recording.

1966

1966 in 1960s music: the full year guide

broadcast

Ready Steady Go! signs off with the Who

ITV broadcast the last Ready Steady Go! on December 23, 1966, per the Rediffusion London archive, with the Who miming to backing tracks they had cut at Ryemuse Sound Studios a week earlier, per thewho.net.

The pop show had run on Friday nights since August 1963, and the clip below is I'm a Boy, one of the two songs the Who recorded for the farewell episode.

concert

Pink Floyd play the opening night of London's UFO Club

Pink Floyd played UFO at the Blarney Club on Tottenham Court Road in London on December 23, 1966, per the band's official gig list, the first night of the club run by Joe Boyd and John Hopkins.

UFO became the home base of London's psychedelic underground, and the clip below is Arnold Layne, the debut single Pink Floyd released in March 1967.

1965 in 1960s music: the full year guide

chart

The Beatles hold No. 1 in Britain with Day Tripper and We Can Work It Out

The Beatles' double A-side Day Tripper / We Can Work It Out was No. 1 on the UK chart dated December 23, 1965, its third week on the chart, per the Official Charts Company.

Cliff Richard's Wind Me Up (Let Me Go) sat at No. 2, and the clip below is Day Tripper.

industry

Astrud Gilberto records with Gil Evans for Look to the Rainbow

Astrud Gilberto recorded Once Upon a Summertime and Berimbau with arranger Gil Evans and his orchestra in New York on December 23, 1965, per the jazzdisco Verve Records discography.

Both tracks appeared on her Verve album Look to the Rainbow, and the clip below is Once Upon a Summertime, a jazz and easy listening standard.

1964

1964 in 1960s music: the full year guide

culture

Brian Wilson breaks down on a flight to Houston

Beach Boys leader Brian Wilson broke down on a flight from Los Angeles to Houston on the morning of December 23, 1964, per the Houston Press.

He soon gave up touring to concentrate on writing and producing, with Glen Campbell filling in on stage, and the clip below is Dance, Dance, Dance, the band's single that season.

concert

The Beatles finish rehearsals for Another Beatles Christmas Show

The Beatles spent December 23, 1964, at the Hammersmith Odeon in London on the third and final day of rehearsals for Another Beatles Christmas Show, with Freddie and the Dreamers, the Yardbirds and Elkie Brooks also on the bill, per the Paul McCartney Project.

The show opened to the public the next night, and the clip below is I Feel Fine, their current single.

Rufus Thomas cuts Can Your Monkey Do the Dog in Memphis

Rufus Thomas recorded Can Your Monkey Do the Dog and I Wanna Get Married in Memphis on December 23, 1963, for release as Stax 144, per the jazzdisco discography of Atlantic and its distributed labels.

It followed his 1963 dance hit Walking the Dog, and the clip below is the soul single from that session.

1962

1962 in 1960s music: the full year guide

concert

The Beatles play night six of their last Star-Club run

The Beatles played the Star-Club in Hamburg on December 23, 1962, the sixth night of their final residency there, per the Paul McCartney Project.

The residency ran to New Year's Eve, and the clip below is P.S. I Love You, the B-side of their first single, Love Me Do.

1961

1961 in 1960s music: the full year guide

concert

The Beatles play an evening show at the Cavern Club

The Beatles played an evening show at the Cavern Club in Liverpool on December 23, 1961, per the Paul McCartney Project.

Brian Epstein had offered to manage them earlier that month, and the clip below is Cry for a Shadow, the instrumental John Lennon and George Harrison recorded in Hamburg that June.

industry

Ike Quebec records Blue and Sentimental at Van Gelder Studio

Tenor saxophonist Ike Quebec recorded the ballad Blue and Sentimental with Grant Green, Sonny Clark, Sam Jones and Louis Hayes at Van Gelder Studio in Englewood Cliffs, New Jersey, on December 23, 1961, per the jazzdisco Van Gelder Studio catalog.

Green, Clark, Jones and Hayes also cut Green's Gooden's Corner that day, unissued until 1979, and the clip below is Blue and Sentimental, the title track of Quebec's Blue Note jazz album.

Teri Thornton starts recording Devil May Care for Riverside

Singer Teri Thornton recorded at Plaza Sound Studios in New York on December 23, 1960, with Clark Terry, Wynton Kelly and Jimmy Cobb, in the first of two sessions for her Riverside album Devil May Care, per the jazzdisco Riverside Records discography.

Norman Simmons arranged and conducted the dates, which finished on January 10, 1961, and the clip below is the album's title track.

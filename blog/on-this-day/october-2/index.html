Elvis Presley releases Return to Sender

RCA Victor released Elvis Presley's Return to Sender, a rock and roll single written by Otis Blackwell and Winfield Scott and featured in his film Girls! Girls! Girls!.

The single sold more than a million copies, became that year's UK Christmas number one, and reached No. 2 on the Billboard Hot 100.

The Beatles begin recording Hello, Goodbye

The Beatles gathered at Studio Two, EMI Studios, from 10pm to 2:30am to lay down the basic track for Paul McCartney's Hello, Goodbye, then known by the working title Hello Hello.

Producer George Martin and engineers Geoff Emerick and Ken Scott oversaw 14 takes of the rhythm track, the start of sessions that produced the group's next international No. 1 single.

1966
concert

The Rolling Stones play two shows at the Gaumont Theatre in Ipswich

The Rolling Stones performed two houses at the Gaumont Theatre in Ipswich, with support from Ike & Tina Turner and the Yardbirds on their 1966 British tour.

The bill paired the Stones with two acts near the height of their own powers, part of a tour that closed out the band's era of small theatre package shows before stadiums took over.

1965
concert

John Coltrane performs A Love Supreme live in Seattle

John Coltrane led an expanded group, his classic quartet plus Pharoah Sanders in his first gig with the band, through a full live performance of A Love Supreme at The Penthouse jazz club in Seattle.

Recorded by local saxophonist Joe Brazil and not discovered until years after his death, the tapes surfaced in 2021 as A Love Supreme: Live in Seattle, one of only two known full live performances of Coltrane's landmark suite, part of the era's 1960s jazz legacy.

concert

The Rolling Stones play two shows at Wigan's ABC Cinema

The Rolling Stones performed at 6:20 and 8:35pm at the ABC Cinema in Wigan, the night after their shows in Chester, deep into their second British tour of the year.

The dates came months after (I Can't Get No) Satisfaction had turned the band into Britain's biggest live draw, packing theatres night after night.

1964
broadcast

The Beatles rehearse for the US show Shindig!

The Beatles gathered at Granville Studio in Fulham Broadway to rehearse three songs, Kansas City, I'm a Loser and Boys, for a British-made segment of the American TV show Shindig!, produced by Jack Good.

The rehearsal set up a live taping in front of fans the very next day, giving American viewers a fresh look at the band just months after Beatlemania first hit the United States.

release

Andrew Oldham Orchestra & Chorus release 16 Hip Hits

Decca's budget Ace of Clubs label issued 16 Hip Hits by the Andrew Oldham Orchestra & Chorus, an album of orchestral covers of current hits arranged by Rolling Stones manager Andrew Loog Oldham.

Rolling Stones guitarist Keith Richards played uncredited guitar on several tracks, including a version of Needles and Pins, blurring the line between the Stones' rock and roll and Oldham's pop orchestrations.

1963
concert

The Rolling Stones play two 10-minute sets at Edmonton's Regal Theatre

The Rolling Stones performed two brief sets at the Regal Theatre in Edmonton, north London, opening for Bo Diddley and the Everly Brothers on their first UK package tour, documented in surviving tour setlists.

Paid about £20 a show as a band, the dates were a grueling proving ground for the Rolling Stones just weeks before their first hit single, Come On, cracked the UK charts.

1962
release

Elvis Presley releases Return to Sender

RCA Victor released Elvis Presley's Return to Sender, a rock and roll single written by Otis Blackwell and Winfield Scott and featured in his film Girls! Girls! Girls!.

The single sold more than a million copies, became that year's UK Christmas number one, and reached No. 2 on the Billboard Hot 100.

1960
broadcast

The McGuire Sisters sing Kids on The Ed Sullivan Show

The McGuire Sisters, joined by the young singing trio the Kane Triplets, performed Kids from the Broadway hit Bye Bye Birdie on CBS's The Ed Sullivan Show.

The appearance carried the sweet vocal-group harmonies that had defined the sisters' fifties hits into primetime television just as rock and roll began reshaping the pop charts.

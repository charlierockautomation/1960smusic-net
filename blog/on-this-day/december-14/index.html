Marvin Gaye's Grapevine reaches No. 1

Marvin Gaye's I Heard It Through the Grapevine was No. 1 on the Billboard Hot 100 dated December 14, 1968, his first pop chart-topper, per the Wikipedia list of 1968 number ones.

Songfacts notes Motown acts held the top three spots that week, with Love Child by Diana Ross and the Supremes and Stevie Wonder's For Once in My Life behind it, and the clip below is the studio recording.

Simon & Garfunkel record I Am a Rock

Simon & Garfunkel recorded a full-band version of I Am a Rock at Columbia Recording Studios in New York on December 14, 1965, per Wikipedia.

Bob Johnston produced it with Hal Blaine on drums, Carol Kaye on bass and Larry Knechtel on organ, the single came out in May 1966 and reached No. 3 on the Hot 100, and the clip below is the recording. Simon & Garfunkel have their own profile.

concert

The Beatles play the Wimbledon Palais for their Southern Area Fan Club

The Beatles played a fan club show at the Wimbledon Palais in London on December 14, 1963, to about 3,000 people according to the NME, per the Paul McCartney Project.

They took the stage at 4 pm for roughly 30 minutes inside a steel cage, it was their only concert at the Palais, and the clip below is the 16mm newsreel of the day.

death

Dinah Washington dies in Detroit

Dinah Washington died at her home in Detroit on December 14, 1963, aged 39, from an accidental overdose, per Wikipedia and the Encyclopedia of Alabama.

She was one of the leading jazz and R&B vocalists of her era, and the clip below is What a Diff'rence a Day Makes.

1962 in 1960s music: the full year guide

concert

The Beatles play the Music Hall in Shrewsbury

The Beatles played the Music Hall in Shrewsbury on December 14, 1962, the first of two shows there, per the Beatles Bible.

The bill also included Robin Hall and Jimmie MacGregor per the 45worlds listing, they returned on April 26, 1963, and the clip below is Love Me Do, their current single.

release

Bob Dylan's first single, Mixed-Up Confusion, is released

Columbia released Bob Dylan's first single, Mixed-Up Confusion backed with Corrina, Corrina, in the United States on December 14, 1962 as Columbia 4-42656, per Wikipedia.

It was recorded on November 14, 1962 at Columbia Studios in New York with a backing band, and the clip below is the single version. Bob Dylan has his own profile.

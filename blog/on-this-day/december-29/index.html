Paul McCartney starts recording Penny Lane at Abbey Road

The Beatles began recording Paul McCartney's Penny Lane in Studio Two at EMI's Abbey Road studios on December 29, 1966, with McCartney recording six takes of piano before overdubbing more piano and harmonium, per the Paul McCartney Project.

The song was released in February 1967 on a double A-side single with Strawberry Fields Forever, and the clip below is Penny Lane from the band's official Vevo channel.

The Grateful Dead play day two of the Miami Pop Festival

The Grateful Dead played the Miami Pop Festival at Gulfstream Park in Hallandale, Florida, on December 29, 1968, the second day of a three-day run, and the Grateful Dead Archive lists Turn On Your Lovelight, Dark Star, St. Stephen, The Eleven, Drums and The Other One from the set.

About 100,000 people attended over the weekend, and the clip below is Dark Star, one of the songs on that list, from the band's Topic channel.

The Jimi Hendrix Experience make their Top of the Pops debut with Hey Joe

The Jimi Hendrix Experience mimed Hey Joe on BBC One's Top of the Pops on December 29, 1966, in an episode introduced by Simon Dee, per the Top of the Pops Archive.

The single had been released on December 16 and went on to reach No. 6 on the UK chart in early 1967, and the clip below is the song from Hendrix's official Vevo channel.

The Beatles play a charity show on the fourth night of Another Beatles Christmas Show

The Beatles played two shows at the Hammersmith Odeon in London on December 29, 1964, the fourth night of Another Beatles Christmas Show, with the Yardbirds on the bill, per the Beatles Bible.

The 8:45 pm performance was billed as The Beatles for Brady, with proceeds going to the Brady Boys' and Girls' Clubs in London's East End, and the clip below is I Feel Fine, their single of that season, from the band's official Vevo channel.

The Weavers give their farewell concert at Orchestra Hall in Chicago

The Weavers played their farewell concert at Orchestra Hall in Chicago on December 29, 1963, and the Chicago History Museum holds photographs of the evening by Raeburn Flerlage.

The folk group had topped the pop charts in 1950 with Goodnight Irene, and the clip below is that song from the group's Topic channel.

1962 in 1960s music: the full year guide

chart

The Tornados spend a second week at No. 1 with Telstar

The Tornados' Telstar held at No. 1 on the Billboard Hot 100 dated December 29, 1962, its second week on top, with Chubby Checker's Limbo Rock at No. 2.

The instrumental, produced by Joe Meek, had entered the chart at No. 85 on November 3 and was the first single by a British group to top the Hot 100, and the clip below is the record from the group's Topic channel.

chart

Ray Charles reaches his peak with You Are My Sunshine

Ray Charles' You Are My Sunshine rose to its No. 7 peak on the Billboard Hot 100 dated December 29, 1962, after entering the chart at No. 77 on November 17.

He had topped the Hot 100 earlier in 1962 with I Can't Stop Loving You, and the clip below is You Are My Sunshine from his official channel.

chart

The 4 Seasons reach their peak with Santa Claus Is Coming to Town

The 4 Seasons' Santa Claus Is Coming to Town rose to its No. 23 peak on the Billboard Hot 100 dated December 29, 1962, after entering the chart at No. 56 on December 15.

The holiday single came the same year as their No. 1 hit Sherry, and the clip below is the song from the Rhino channel.

chart

The Cookies reach their peak with Chains

The Cookies' Chains rose to its No. 17 peak on the Billboard Hot 100 dated December 29, 1962, after entering the chart at No. 87 on November 10.

The Beatles would cover the song on their 1963 debut album Please Please Me, and the clip below is the original from the Cookies' Topic channel.

Paul and Paula debut on the Hot 100 with Hey Paula

Paul and Paula's Hey Paula entered the Billboard Hot 100 dated December 29, 1962 at No. 96.

The single would climb to No. 1 in February 1963, and the clip below is the song from the duo's Topic channel.

The Crystals debut on the Hot 100 with He's Sure the Boy I Love

The Crystals' He's Sure the Boy I Love entered the Billboard Hot 100 dated December 29, 1962 at No. 93.

The single was a Phil Spector production, and the clip below is the song from the Phil Spector Vevo channel.

Ned Miller debuts on the Hot 100 with From a Jack to a King

The Beatles play the eleventh night of their last Star-Club residency

The Beatles played the Star-Club in Hamburg on December 29, 1962, the eleventh date of their third and final residency there, per the Paul McCartney Project.

Their only single out at the time was Love Me Do, released in the UK on October 5, 1962, and the clip below is that song from the band's Topic channel.

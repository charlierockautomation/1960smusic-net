The Who play Fairfield Halls behind Tommy

The Who played Fairfield Halls in Croydon on September 21, 1969, running through cuts from their new rock opera Tommy alongside earlier hits like My Generation and I Can't Explain, according to Setlist.fm.

The show came in the middle of a triumphant UK run that turned the Who's ambitious concept album into a genuine live sensation.

concert

The Great Speckled Bird throws a free concert in Piedmont Park

The underground Atlanta newspaper the Great Speckled Bird sponsored a free outdoor concert at Piedmont Park on September 21, 1969, with the Hampton Grease Band, Booger Band, Brickwall, Radar, and Sweet Younguns on the bill, as the Digital Library of Georgia's dated photo archive confirms.

The show captured the grassroots counterculture scene taking root in the South even as the era's biggest festivals grabbed the national headlines.

Jeannie C. Riley tops the Hot 100 with Harper Valley P.T.A.

Jeannie C. Riley's Harper Valley P.T.A. reached number one on the Billboard Hot 100 on September 21, 1968, then climbed to the top of the Hot Country Singles chart the following week, per Wikipedia.

Riley became the first woman to top both charts with the same single, a Tom T. Hall story song that helped drive the era's crossover into country music.

chart

Deep Purple's Hush peaks at number four

Deep Purple's cover of Hush, originally a Joe South song recorded by Billy Joe Royal, peaked at number four on the Billboard Hot 100 on September 21, 1968, per Wikipedia.

The single introduced American listeners to Jon Lord's overdriven Hammond organ and Ritchie Blackmore's guitar work months before the band pivoted toward the heavier sound that would define hard rock.

The Velvet Underground stage a Boston happening

The Velvet Underground played a preview performance at Boston's Savoy Theatre on September 21, 1967, a night ahead of the official opening of their multimedia "Happening" run built around a screening of the film The Happening, as the UMass Amherst archives record.

Backed by the Great Eastern light show, the band's dark, feedback-driven set introduced Boston audiences to the art-rock sound of their just-released debut album, a defining record of psychedelic rock.

The Beatles film Blue Jay Way at West Malling

The Beatles filmed the surreal Blue Jay Way sequence for Magical Mystery Tour inside a fog-filled hangar at West Malling air station in Kent on September 21, 1967, as The Beatles Bible records.

George Harrison mimed his own composition amid dry ice and disorienting camera effects, one of the strangest scenes in the Beatles' catalog of film work.

The Beach Boys finish Good Vibrations

Brian Wilson and the Beach Boys completed the final vocal overdubs for Good Vibrations on September 21, 1966, closing out a marathon session that had run since February across four different Hollywood studios, as Wikipedia details.

The nearly seven-month, $16,000 production became the most expensive single of its era and pushed the band deep into the experimental territory that would soon ripple through garage and surf rock.

The Moody Blues play Brian Epstein's Royal Festival Hall bill

The Moody Blues, freshly signed to Brian Epstein's management roster, played An Evening of Popular British Music at London's Royal Festival Hall on September 21, 1965, as part of the Commonwealth Arts Festival, according to The Paul McCartney Project.

It was the band's first major date under Epstein's guidance, a sign of the rising stature they carried into the British Invasion just months after their debut hit Go Now.

concert

The Who borrow gear for a wild night in The Hague

The Who played De Marathon in The Hague on September 21, 1965, borrowing amplifiers from a local band called the Empty Hearts after Keith Moon had thrown his own drum kit out of a hotel window the night before, per De Jongenskamer.

The band tore through early staples like My Generation and Substitute without breaking any of the borrowed gear, a rare quiet night by the Who's destructive standards.

The Beatles fly home to a hero's welcome

The Beatles flew back into London Airport at 9:35 p.m. on September 21, 1964, closing out their first full North American tour, as The Beatles Bible records.

Thousands of fans crowded the rooftop of the Queen's Building to catch a glimpse of the Beatles, a homecoming welcome that showed how far Beatlemania had spread in a single month abroad.

Bobby Vinton's Blue Velvet hits number one

Bobby Vinton's lush cover of Blue Velvet reached number one on the Billboard Hot 100 on September 21, 1963, holding the top spot for four weeks, as Wikipedia confirms.

The traditional ballad marked one of the last major chart triumphs for a crooner-style hit before the British Invasion swept away the pop and Brill Building sound just months later.

The Beatles play Liverpool twice in one day

The Beatles played a lunchtime set at the Cavern Club in Liverpool on September 21, 1961, then returned that evening for their third Litherland Town Hall date of the month, sharing the bill with Gerry and the Pacemakers and Rory Storm and the Hurricanes, as The Beatles Bible records.

The double booking captured the raw momentum of Liverpool's beat scene more than a year before the Beatles signed their first recording contract, the same circuit that would soon feed the British Invasion.

Frequently Asked Questions

What happened in 1960s music on September 21?

September 21 produced 12 verified music events across the decade, headlined by the Beach Boys wrapping up the marathon Good Vibrations sessions in 1966.

The same date saw the Beatles play a lunchtime and evening show in Liverpool in 1961, film the surreal Blue Jay Way sequence in 1967, and fly home from their first US tour in 1964, while Jeannie C. Riley and Deep Purple both hit the Billboard Hot 100 in 1968.

How long did it take to record Good Vibrations?

Brian Wilson worked on Good Vibrations from February 17 to September 21, 1966, spread across four different Hollywood studios including Gold Star and Sunset Sound.

The finished single did not reach stores until October 10, 1966, making the roughly seven-month production one of the most expensive and complex pop singles of the decade.

Did the Beatles really play two shows in one day at the Cavern Club and Litherland Town Hall?

Yes.

On September 21, 1961, the Beatles played a lunchtime set at the Cavern Club in Liverpool, then performed that same evening at Litherland Town Hall alongside Gerry and the Pacemakers and Rory Storm and the Hurricanes, and double and even triple bookings were common for the band during their busy Merseyside club years before they signed a recording contract.

What was Harper Valley P.T.A. about and did it top both the pop and country charts?

Harper Valley P.T.A., written by Tom T. Hall, tells the story of a widowed mother who confronts her small town's hypocritical school board.

Jeannie C. Riley's recording topped the Billboard Hot 100 on September 21, 1968, then reached number one on the Hot Country Singles chart the following week, making her the first woman to top both charts with the same song.

What scene was filmed for Blue Jay Way in Magical Mystery Tour?

The Beatles filmed George Harrison miming his song Blue Jay Way inside a fog machine-filled hangar at West Malling air station in Kent on September 21, 1967.

The hazy, disorienting footage became one of the most surreal sequences in the Magical Mystery Tour television special.

Why did the Who have to borrow equipment for their 1965 show in the Netherlands?

Keith Moon threw his own drum kit out of a hotel window in Delft the night before the Who's September 21, 1965 show at De Marathon in The Hague.

A local band called the Empty Hearts lent the Who their gear for the night, and the show went off without any of the smashed instruments the Who were already known for.

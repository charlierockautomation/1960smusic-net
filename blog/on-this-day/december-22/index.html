The Tornados' Telstar becomes a British first at No. 1 in America

The Tornados' Telstar was No. 1 on the Billboard Hot 100 dated December 22, 1962, replacing the 4 Seasons' Big Girls Don't Cry.

Produced by Joe Meek and named for the communications satellite launched that July, it was the first American No. 1 by a British group, a forerunner of the British Invasion, and the clip below is the original recording.

John Lennon and Yoko Ono testify to Canada's drug inquiry

The Beatles' John Lennon and Yoko Ono gave private testimony to Canada's Le Dain Commission on the non-medical use of drugs on December 22, 1969, meeting it in their private railway car at Montreal's CNR station, per the Ottawa Beatles Site.

The next day they met Prime Minister Pierre Trudeau in Ottawa as part of their peace campaign, and the clip below is Give Peace a Chance, recorded in Montreal during their bed-in that June.

1967 in 1960s music: the full year guide

festival

Pink Floyd and Jimi Hendrix play Christmas on Earth Continued

Pink Floyd, the Jimi Hendrix Experience, Eric Burdon and the Animals, the Move and Soft Machine were billed at Christmas on Earth Continued, an all-night party at London's Olympia on December 22, 1967, per the Bradford Timeline concert listings.

It is remembered as one of Syd Barrett's last major shows with Pink Floyd, and the clip below is See Emily Play, his psychedelic 1967 single, from the band's official channel.

Tom Jones holds No. 1 in Britain with Green, Green Grass of Home

Tom Jones's Green, Green Grass of Home was No. 1 on the UK chart dated December 22, 1966, its seventh week on the chart, per the Official Charts Company.

The Seekers' Morningtown Ride sat at No. 2, and the clip below is Jones's recording of the Curly Putman country song.

industry

George Martin splices two takes into Strawberry Fields Forever

At EMI Studios on December 22, 1966, George Martin and engineer Geoff Emerick joined the start of take 7 to the end of take 26 of the Beatles' Strawberry Fields Forever and mixed the result in mono, per the Paul McCartney Project.

The edit finished John Lennon's song nearly a month after work began on November 24, and the clip below is the Beatles' official film for it.

Ernest Tubb cuts Hello Trouble in Nashville

Ernest Tubb and his Texas Troubadours recorded Hello Trouble (Come On In) and Just One More at RCA Victor's studio in Nashville on December 22, 1965, per the Discography of American Historical Recordings.

Tubb had been a Decca country star since the early 1940s, and the clip below is his signature song, Walking the Floor Over You.

The Beatles rehearse Another Beatles Christmas Show

The Beatles spent December 22, 1964, at the Hammersmith Odeon in London on the second of three days of rehearsals for Another Beatles Christmas Show, per the Paul McCartney Project.

Photographer Terry O'Neill shot press pictures of the cast that day, and the clip below is She's a Woman, the B-side of their current single, I Feel Fine.

The Beatles bring their Christmas Show preview home to Liverpool

The Beatles played the Empire Theatre in Liverpool on December 22, 1963, the second preview of The Beatles Christmas Show with Cilla Black, Billy J. Kramer with the Dakotas, the Fourmost and Tommy Quickly, per the Paul McCartney Project.

The London run opened at the Finsbury Park Astoria on December 24, and the clip below is All My Loving from With the Beatles, released a month earlier.

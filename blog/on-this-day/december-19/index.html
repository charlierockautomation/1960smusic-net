The Supremes reach No. 1 with Come See About Me

The Supremes' Come See About Me reached No. 1 on the Billboard Hot 100 dated December 19, 1964, replacing Bobby Vinton's Mr. Lonely, per Wikipedia's list of 1964 number ones.

It was the third of their five straight No. 1 hits, per Wikipedia, the Beatles' I Feel Fine replaced it a week later, and the clip below is the Supremes' official lyric video.

The Beatles send fans their seventh and last Christmas record

The Beatles' Seventh Christmas Record, subtitled Happy Christmas 1969, was issued to fan-club members on December 19, 1969, as Lyntone LYN 1970/1971, per the Lyntone catalogue at jpgr.co.uk.

It closed the run of seven flexi discs that began in 1963, per Wikipedia, and the clip below is Come Together from Abbey Road, the album the group had released that fall.

The Grateful Dead open a Fillmore show with an acoustic set

The Grateful Dead played the Fillmore Auditorium in San Francisco on December 19, 1969, opening with Jerry Garcia and Bob Weir on acoustic guitars for Monkey and the Engineer and Little Sadie, per the Grateful Dead Archive Online.

The electric set that followed ran to Uncle John's Band, still unreleased at the time, and the clip below is the studio version from Workingman's Dead on the band's official channel.

Bob Dylan plays Santa Monica Civic Auditorium

Bob Dylan played the Santa Monica Civic Auditorium in California on December 19, 1965, the night after Pasadena, per Olof Björner's Still on the Road concert chronology.

It is the last 1965 show in that chronology, closing a year of acoustic-then-electric sets, and the clip below is Like a Rolling Stone, his hit of that summer.

The Beatles hold the top two places on the UK chart

The Beatles' I Want to Hold Your Hand was No. 1 and She Loves You No. 2 on the UK chart dated December 19, 1963, per the Official Charts Company.

Glad All Over by The Dave Clark Five climbed from No. 9 to No. 4 that week, and the clip below is She Loves You.

1962 in 1960s music: the full year guide

concert

The Beatles play night two of their last Star-Club run

The Beatles played the Star-Club in Hamburg on December 19, 1962, the second night of their final residency there, per the Paul McCartney Project.

Back home their debut single Love Me Do was in its 11th week on the UK chart dated December 20, per the Official Charts Company, and the clip below is its B-side, P.S. I Love You.

1961 in 1960s music: the full year guide

concert

The Beatles play a Cavern Club lunchtime show

The Beatles played a lunchtime session at the Cavern Club in Liverpool on December 19, 1961, per the Paul McCartney Project.

Two weeks later, on January 1, 1962, they auditioned for Decca in London, and the clip below is Cry for a Shadow, the instrumental John Lennon and George Harrison recorded in Hamburg that June.

Are You Lonesome Tonight? holds No. 1 for Elvis Presley

Elvis Presley's Are You Lonesome Tonight? spent a fourth straight week at No. 1 on the Billboard Hot 100 dated December 19, 1960, per Wikipedia's list of 1960 number ones.

It stayed on top through January 2, 1961, six weeks in all, and the clip below is Elvis's official audio.

industry

Frank Sinatra starts his first sessions for Reprise

Frank Sinatra began recording Ring-a-Ding-Ding!, the first album for his own Reprise label, in Los Angeles on December 19, 1960, with sessions running to December 21, per CastAlbums.org.

Johnny Mandel arranged and conducted the album, and the clip below is its title song.

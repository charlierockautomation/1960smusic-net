JFK delivers his We Choose to Go to the Moon speech

President John F. Kennedy addressed a crowd of roughly 40,000 people at Rice University's stadium in Houston, laying out the case for landing an American on the Moon before the decade was out, per the JFK Library.

The speech's space-age optimism fed straight into the reverb-drenched instrumentals of the era's garage and surf rock scene, where songs chasing that same futuristic thrill were already climbing the charts.

1969

The Rolling Stones release Through the Past, Darkly (Big Hits Vol. 2)

Decca Records released the Rolling Stones' second hits compilation, Through the Past, Darkly (Big Hits Vol. 2), in a distinctive octagonal gatefold sleeve carrying an inside epitaph to founding guitarist Brian Jones, who had died two months earlier, per Wikipedia.

The album reached number two on the UK chart and included the band's new summer single Honky Tonk Women, the last Rolling Stones recording to feature Jones.

1968
concert

The Yardbirds, soon to become Led Zeppelin, play Stockholm's Gröna Lund

Jimmy Page's new lineup, still touring under a leftover Yardbirds contract, opened the Swedish leg of their first tour with a show on the Stora Scenen stage at Gröna Lund amusement park, the first time Page, Robert Plant, John Paul Jones, and John Bonham played together outside England, per Wikipedia.

The band renamed itself Led Zeppelin weeks later, and the short Scandinavian run became the testing ground for the blues-heavy material that filled their debut album.

industry

The Beatles overdub lead vocals onto Glass Onion

With producer George Martin away, his young assistant Chris Thomas supervised the Beatles as John Lennon double-tracked his lead vocal and Ringo Starr added tambourine onto the Glass Onion rhythm track cut the night before, per The Beatles Bible.

The overnight session, running past 3am, pushed Lennon's sly, self-referential White Album track closer to its finished shape.

1967
concert

Traffic play a Swedish-radio-recorded show at Stockholm's Konserthuset

The newly formed Traffic, only months removed from Steve Winwood leaving the Spencer Davis Group, played Stockholm's Konserthuset with Sveriges Radio recording the set, one of the earliest live Traffic broadcasts to survive, per setlist.fm.

The show came three months before their debut album Mr. Fantasy, built on the psychedelic rock sound of their breakthrough single Paper Sun.

1966
broadcast

The Monkees premieres on NBC

The Monkees, a television sitcom about a fictional pop band modeled on the freewheeling style of the Beatles' A Hard Day's Night, made its broadcast debut on NBC in prime time, per Songfacts.

The show turned Micky Dolenz, Davy Jones, Michael Nesmith, and Peter Tork into a real chart-topping act within weeks, part of the same Brill Building-era pop machine, starting with Last Train to Clarksville.

broadcast

The Roger Miller Show premieres on NBC

Country singer-songwriter Roger Miller's own musical variety series premiered on NBC in the Monday 8:30pm slot, built around his comic songwriting and country guest stars, per Wikipedia.

The show ran only through December, but it captured Miller near the commercial peak of hits like King of the Road that had made him a rare 1960s country crossover star on national television.

1965
broadcast

The Beatles' final live Ed Sullivan Show appearance airs

CBS broadcast the Beatles' fourth and last appearance on The Ed Sullivan Show, a six-song set taped a month earlier the day before their Shea Stadium concert, including Paul McCartney's first televised solo performance of Yesterday backed by a string quartet, per The Beatles Bible.

It closed out the run of live Beatles performances on the same program that had helped launch Beatlemania in America back in February 1964.

release

Simon & Garfunkel's electric Sound of Silence is released as a single

Columbia Records released an overdubbed electric version of Simon & Garfunkel's Sound of Silence as a single, adding drums, bass, and electric guitar to the original acoustic track without telling Paul Simon or Art Garfunkel, who had already split up as a duo, per Wikipedia.

The new version caught the rising folk rock wave and climbed to number one on the Billboard Hot 100 by January 1966, reuniting the pair and launching their career.

1964
concert

The Beatles play Boston Garden

The Beatles played their only Boston Garden concert of the decade to nearly 14,000 fans, landing in the early hours of the morning at Hanscom Field to dodge the crowds waiting at Boston's main airport, per The Beatles Bible.

The stop came midway through their first full North American tour behind A Hard Day's Night, with screaming fans drowning out most of the set.

1963
chart

She Loves You reaches number one on the UK singles chart

The Beatles' She Loves You topped the UK Singles Chart for the first of its two separate runs at number one, powered by Ringo Starr's driving beat and the band's yeah, yeah, yeah refrain, per Official Charts.

It went on to become the best-selling single in Britain for all of 1963 and remains the highest-selling single of the Beatles' career in the UK.

culture

Norman Parkinson photographs the Beatles at Abbey Road

Fashion photographer Norman Parkinson, invited by Queen magazine, spent the day shooting black-and-white portraits of the Beatles at the President Hotel and inside Abbey Road's Studio Two, where the band was recording Don't Bother Me, Little Child, and I Wanna Be Your Man for With the Beatles, per Flashbak.

His relaxed, high-fashion images of the group at work became some of the most widely reproduced early photographs of the band.

1962
industry

Coleman Hawkins records I Remember You for Desafinado

Tenor saxophonist Coleman Hawkins cut his bossa nova arrangement of the standard I Remember You at Rudy Van Gelder's New Jersey studio, backed by Tommy Flanagan on claves, guitarists Howard Collins and Barry Galbraith, bassist Major Holley, and drummer Eddie Locke, per All About Jazz.

The track landed on Desafinado, Hawkins' entry into the bossa nova wave that swept American jazz and easy listening in 1962, arranged by Manny Albam for Impulse! Records.

Frequently Asked Questions

What happened in 1960s music on September 12?

September 12 saw 13 verified music events across the decade, headlined by JFK's We Choose to Go to the Moon speech at Rice University in 1962.

The same date also produced She Loves You hitting UK number one, The Monkees' NBC premiere, and the first Scandinavian show by the band that became Led Zeppelin.

When did She Loves You reach number one in the UK?

The Beatles' She Loves You topped the UK Singles Chart on September 12, 1963, the first of two separate runs at number one.

It became the best-selling UK single of 1963 and remains the band's highest-selling single in Britain.

When did The Monkees TV show premiere?

The Monkees premiered on NBC on September 12, 1966.

Within weeks the fictional TV band became a real chart-topping act, led off by the single Last Train to Clarksville.

What was JFK's We Choose to Go to the Moon speech about?

On September 12, 1962, President Kennedy addressed roughly 40,000 people at Rice University's stadium in Houston to build public support for landing an American on the Moon before 1970.

The speech's futuristic optimism echoed through the reverb-heavy instrumentals of the period's surf and garage rock scene.

When did Led Zeppelin play their first show outside England?

On September 12, 1968, Jimmy Page's new band, still billed as the Yardbirds under a leftover contract, played Stockholm's Gröna Lund amusement park.

It was the first time Page, Robert Plant, John Paul Jones, and John Bonham performed together outside England, weeks before renaming themselves Led Zeppelin.

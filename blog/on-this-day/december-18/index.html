The Tokens take The Lion Sleeps Tonight to No. 1

The Tokens' The Lion Sleeps Tonight reached No. 1 on the Billboard Hot 100 dated December 18, 1961, replacing The Marvelettes' Please Mr. Postman, per Wikipedia's list of 1961 number ones.

The song grew from Solomon Linda's South African recording Mbube, Elvis Presley's Blue Hawaii topped the album chart the same week, and the clip below is the Tokens' official audio.

Katharine Hepburn opens on Broadway in Coco

Coco, the musical about the designer Coco Chanel with music by André Previn and lyrics by Alan Jay Lerner, opened at the Mark Hellinger Theatre in New York on December 18, 1969, per IBDB.

Katharine Hepburn played the title role and earned a Tony nomination, the show ran 329 performances, and the clip below is Hepburn singing Always Mademoiselle from the cast album.

John Lennon and Yoko Ono perform inside a white bag at the Royal Albert Hall

John Lennon and Yoko Ono climbed into a large white bag on stage at the Royal Albert Hall in London on December 18, 1968, at the Arts Lab's Underground Christmas party, per the Beatles Bible.

Billed as Alchemical Wedding, it was an early outing for what the couple soon called bagism, and the clip below is The Ballad of John and Yoko, the 1969 Beatles single that sings of them eating chocolate cake in a bag.

chart

The Scaffold hold No. 1 in the UK with Lily the Pink

The Scaffold's Lily the Pink was No. 1 for a second week on the UK chart dated December 18, 1968, ahead of Nina Simone's Ain't Got No, I Got Life, per the Official Charts Company.

The Liverpool trio included Mike McGear, Paul McCartney's younger brother, and the clip below is Lily the Pink from the group's official Topic channel.

culture

Chitty Chitty Bang Bang has its New York premiere

The musical film Chitty Chitty Bang Bang, starring Dick Van Dyke with songs by Richard M. Sherman and Robert B. Sherman, had its New York premiere on December 18, 1968, per the AFI Catalog.

The AFI Catalog notes the premiere raised money for the Boys Club of Queens, and the clip below is Van Dyke singing the title song, from Turner Classic Movies.

1967 in 1960s music: the full year guide

release

The Beach Boys release Wild Honey

The Beach Boys released Wild Honey, their 13th studio album, on Capitol Records on December 18, 1967, alongside the single Darlin', per Wikipedia, citing Keith Badman's Beach Boys diary.

Recorded at Brian Wilson's home studio after the Smile project was shelved, it was the band's first turn toward soul music, and the clip below is the title track from the Beach Boys' official channel.

1965 in 1960s music: the full year guide

chart

Turn! Turn! Turn! holds No. 1 as Whipped Cream tops the albums

The Byrds' Turn! Turn! Turn! spent its third and final week at No. 1 on the Billboard Hot 100 dated December 18, 1965, per Wikipedia's list of 1965 number ones.

Herb Alpert's Tijuana Brass held No. 1 on the album chart that week with Whipped Cream & Other Delights, home of A Taste of Honey, and the clip below is Turn! Turn! Turn!

1964

1964 in 1960s music: the full year guide

death

Chicago fans crowd Sam Cooke's first funeral service

Sam Cooke's first funeral service was held at A. R. Leak Funeral Home in Chicago on December 18, 1964, a week after he was shot dead in Los Angeles, per Wikipedia, citing Ebony.

The Chicago Defender reported both chapels filled soon after the doors opened, per Rock's Backpages, and the clip below is A Change Is Gonna Come, issued as a B-side after his death.

release

The Beatles send fans Another Beatles Christmas Record

The Beatles' second fan-club Christmas flexi disc, Another Beatles Christmas Record, was issued on December 18, 1964, as Lyntone LYN 757, per the Lyntone catalogue at jpgr.co.uk.

It was recorded at Abbey Road that October 26 and opens with the group singing Jingle Bells, and the clip below is No Reply, the opening track of Beatles for Sale, out two weeks earlier.

1963 in 1960s music: the full year guide

The Beatles record their first BBC holiday special, From Us To You

The Beatles recorded the BBC radio special From Us To You at the Paris Theatre in London on December 18, 1963, per the Paul McCartney Project.

The show aired on Boxing Day, December 26, and the clip below is I Want to Hold Your Hand, their newest single, released in Britain on November 29.

1962

1962 in 1960s music: the full year guide

concert

The Beatles open their last Star-Club residency in Hamburg

The Beatles began their third and final residency at the Star-Club in Hamburg on December 18, 1962, a run of 13 nights through New Year's Eve, per the Paul McCartney Project.

Tapes from the stand came out in 1977 as Live! at the Star-Club, and the clip below is Please Please Me, the single they had cut in London that November.

concert

Shostakovich's Babi Yar symphony premieres in Moscow

Dmitri Shostakovich's Symphony No. 13, Babi Yar, had its premiere at the Moscow Conservatory's Great Hall on December 18, 1962, under Kirill Kondrashin, per Wikipedia.

The work sets Yevgeny Yevtushenko's poems, including one on the Babi Yar massacre, went ahead despite official pressure, and the clip below is its opening movement by the Moscow Philharmonic Orchestra.

Sam Cooke records Twistin' the Night Away

Sam Cooke recorded Twistin' the Night Away at RCA's studio in Hollywood on December 18, 1961, per Wikipedia.

RCA released it on January 9, 1962, it reached No. 9 on the Hot 100 and No. 1 on the R&B chart, and the clip below is the single.

concert

The Beatles play a Cavern Club lunchtime show

The Beatles played a lunchtime session at the Cavern Club in Liverpool on December 18, 1961, per the Paul McCartney Project.

The club's DJ Bob Wooler counted 292 Beatles appearances there between 1961 and 1963, and the clip below is Ain't She Sweet, which they had recorded in Hamburg that June.

death

Bandleader Leo Reisman dies at 64

Violinist and bandleader Leo Reisman died at his home in Manhattan on December 18, 1961, per the New York Public Library archives, which cite his New York Times obituary.

His dance orchestra was a big name of the 1920s and 1930s, and the clip below is its recording of Cole Porter's Night and Day.
